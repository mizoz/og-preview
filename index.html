    What is Open Graph?
    
    
    Open Graph is a small set of <meta> tags, started by Facebook in 2010, that tells apps what to show when someone shares your link: the title, a short description, an image and the URL. LinkedIn, Slack, Discord, iMessage, WhatsApp and most other apps read the same tags. X reads them too, but wants one extra tag, twitter:card, to decide which card to draw. Without these tags, apps guess from your page, and the guess is usually a random image or none at all.
    

    
    Required Open Graph tags
    
    
    
    
    
      
    TagWhat it doesExample
    
      
        
    og:titleHeadline on the cardHow we cut check-in time in half
    
        
    og:descriptionOne or two lines under itWhat we changed at our last three events
    
        
    og:imageThe picture. Absolute https URL.https://example.com/share.png
    
        
    og:urlThe canonical address of the pagehttps://example.com/blog/check-in
    
        
    og:typeKind of page, usually website or articlearticle
    
        
    twitter:cardWhich card X showssummary_large_image
    
        
    <link rel="canonical">The one true URL, for search engineshttps://example.com/blog/check-in
    
          
    
    
    
    
    
    Nice to have: og:site_name, og:image:width and og:image:height (Facebook can show the image on the very first share), og:image:alt, og:locale, and theme-color (Discord uses it for the embed bar).

    FAQ
    
    
    
      
    What size should an og:image be in 2026?
    1200 x 630 pixels (1.91:1), as a PNG or JPG under 5 MB. That works everywhere: it meets LinkedIn's 1200 x 627 minimum, Facebook's 1200 x 630 recommendation, and X crops it only slightly for its large card. Keep text and logos away from the edges.
    
      
    Why does my link show the old image or title after I changed it?
    Every platform caches the preview the first time someone shares the link. Re-scrape the URL in LinkedIn Post Inspector and the Facebook Sharing Debugger. For Slack, X, Discord and WhatsApp there is no public refresh button, so wait for the cache to expire or share the URL with a harmless query string like ?v=2.
    
      
    Why can't this tool fetch my page directly?
    Browsers block a web page from reading another site's HTML unless that site allows it (CORS). Platform crawlers run on servers, so they don't have this limit. Paste the HTML instead, or use the bookmarklet, which reads the tags from the page you are on and opens them here.
    
      
    Do I need both Open Graph and Twitter card tags?
    You need Open Graph tags plus one twitter:card tag. X falls back to og:title, og:description and og:image, so twitter:title and twitter:image are optional. Without twitter:card, X may not show a card at all.
    
      
    Can og:image be a relative URL?
    No. Use a full absolute URL starting with https://. Crawlers often don't resolve relative paths, so /images/share.png can end up as no image at all.
    
      
    How long should the title and description be?
    Google cuts titles at about 600 pixels wide, roughly 50 to 60 characters, and descriptions after about two lines, roughly 150 to 160 characters. Social cards show even less, often one or two lines, so put the important words first.
    
      
    Are these previews exact?
    No. They are close lookalikes, not official renderings, and the platforms change their layouts often. Use them to catch problems fast, then confirm with each platform's own debugger before a big launch.
    
      
    Does this tool send my HTML anywhere?
    No. Parsing and previews happen in your browser. The only network requests are the ones you ask for: fetching the URL you type, and loading the og:image so its size can be measured.
